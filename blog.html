Shopify, single page or custom: which website fits your business?

A quick guide to picking the right kind of website before you spend any money.

Most businesses don't need the most expensive website. They need the one that matches what they sell and how they sell it. Here is how we help clients choose.

Single page website

Best for a new business, an event, a single product or a service with one clear call to action. Everything lives on one scrolling page: who you are, what you offer, proof and a way to get in touch. It is quick to launch and easy to keep up to date.

Shopify store

Best when you sell products online. Shopify takes care of payments, stock, shipping and security, so you can focus on products and marketing. We handle the theme, product setup and the apps you need.

Custom website

Best when your business has its own workflow: bookings, listings, member areas, multiple languages or links to other systems. A custom build costs more up front but fits exactly how you work.

Our rule of thumb

  • Just need to be found and contacted? Start with a single page.
  • Selling products? Go Shopify.
  • Unique process or features? Go custom.

Not sure? Tell us about your business and we'll recommend the simplest option that works.

7 things to check before your website goes live

The small details that make a big difference on launch day.

Launch day is exciting, and it is easy to miss the basics. Run through this list before you share your new site with the world.

  1. Test on real phones. Most visitors will see your site on a phone first. Check every page, menu and form on at least one iPhone and one Android.
  2. Check your contact details. Phone numbers, WhatsApp links and email addresses should all work with one tap.
  3. Turn on SSL. The padlock in the address bar builds trust, and browsers warn visitors away from sites without it.
  4. Set page titles and descriptions. These are what people see in Google results, so make them clear and specific.
  5. Compress your images. Large photos are the most common reason a site feels slow.
  6. Connect analytics. You can't improve what you don't measure.
  7. Plan your backups. Know where your backups are and how to restore them before you need them.

Why a monthly marketing plan beats posting when you remember

Consistency, not luck, is what grows an audience.

Many small businesses post on social media when they have time, run an ad when sales are slow and then stop again. The result is an audience that forgets you between posts.

What a monthly plan gives you

  • A content calendar, so posts go out on a steady rhythm instead of in bursts.
  • Clear goals for each month: more enquiries, more store visits or a product launch.
  • Ads that learn. Paid campaigns on Meta, Google and TikTok get better the longer they run with steady budgets.
  • A monthly report that shows what worked, so next month's plan is better than this one.

Start small

You don't need to be everywhere. Pick the one or two platforms where your customers already spend time, post consistently and review the numbers every month.
